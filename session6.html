What does the Hormuz closure cost?

When a crisis breaks, a minister wants a number by the evening. In this session you cost the 2026 closure of the Strait of Hormuz for any country, give a range instead of a falsely precise figure, and check an AI assistant's answer against your own.

About 3 hours at your own paceNo software needed; an AI assistant of your choice for one stepYour answers stay in this browser only
    Step 1 · Puzzle · 10 min

    The Hormuz shock

    About a fifth of the world's oil normally passes through the Strait of Hormuz. Satellite tracking of ships shows what happened in 2026.

    Your guess

    For a typical EU country, what is the yearly cost of the oil and gas price rise that followed, as a share of GDP?

    Step 2 · Lesson · 25 min

    Costing a shock

    The first-round cost

    When energy prices jump, an importing country pays more for the same oil and gas. That extra bill, as a share of GDP, is the first-round cost:

    cost (% of GDP) ≈ net energy imports (% of GDP) × price rise (%) × (1 − demand response)

    • Net energy imports: fuel imports minus fuel exports (oil, gas, coal), as a share of GDP. A net exporter such as Norway gains when prices rise.
    • Price rise: Brent crude averaged $69 a barrel in 2025. From March to September 2026 it averaged about $100 (+44%), with a peak of $138 on 7 April (+100%).
    • Demand response: in the short run, people and firms cut their use only a little, perhaps by up to 10%.

    Why a range

    Every input is uncertain: how long the closure lasts, how high prices stay, how much demand adjusts. Give a low case and a high case, and say what each assumes. A single number to two decimals signals precision you do not have.

    What the first round leaves out

    Second-round effects (higher inflation, central-bank reactions, lost exports to Gulf customers) and physical shortages, which hit Asian importers of Gulf oil hardest. Say so in your note.

    Reading PortWatch

    PortWatch counts ships from their automatic identification system (AIS) signals. In a war zone, ships switch their transponders off, so counts understate real traffic: the session 1 question, “who produced this number and how?”, again.

    Check yourself

    Germany's net energy imports are 1.67% of GDP. With a 44% price rise and no demand response, what is the first-round cost, % of GDP (two decimals)?

    Step 3 · Activity · 20 min

    Beat the AI

    1. Your estimate

    Low case: price +44%, demand falls 10%. High case: price +100% (the April peak sustained), no demand response. Compute both, % of GDP, two decimals.

    2. Ask an AI assistant

    Copy this prompt into any AI assistant, changing nothing but the country, and note its answer.

    Step 4 · Feedback · 10 min

    Who was closer?

    For a typical EU country, with net energy imports of about 2% of GDP, the first-round cost lies between about 0.8% (low case) and 2% of GDP (high case) a year. South Korea, Thailand and Malta are among the most exposed; Norway and Saudi Arabia are net exporters. Saudi Arabia's gain is theoretical while its tankers cannot leave the Gulf: pipelines to the Red Sea and, for the United Arab Emirates, to the Gulf of Oman carry only part of its exports.

    Audit the AI's answer

    AI assistants are fluent and fast, and useful for a first structure. They often state numbers without sources, use outdated prices, or invent references. Treat them as a junior colleague whose every number you check, never as an oracle.

    Step 5 · Put it together · optional, 45 min

    A two-page note for a minister

    You now have every method an analyst needs for a short policy note. To practise, pick one country and one question, and write two pages on your own. Four questions that use the course's methods:

    • Is it spending enough on defence, and would voters accept more? (sessions 1, 2, 4 and this one)
    • Should it reduce its dependence on China for a critical product? (sessions 3 and this one)
    • Are sanctions on Russia being circumvented through its trade? (sessions 1 and 5)
    • Can we trust its official growth figures? (sessions 1 and 4)

    Whatever the question, a good note contains six elements: a headline that gives the answer in three lines; one to three honest charts; one estimate stated in words; a range with a low and a high case; what could make the answer wrong; and its sources. Use this grid to check your own work.

    CriterionExcellentInsufficient
    QuestionMade precise for your country, relevant to a decision-makerVague, or not answered
    SourceChosen for a reason; reliability, coverage and bias discussedTaken as given
    ChartHonest scale and units, readable in 10 seconds, sourcedMisleading or decorative
    EstimateMethod fits the question; result in words with a rangeNumbers without interpretation
    What could make us wrongNames the key assumption and how its failure would change the conclusionMissing or generic
    RecommendationFollows from the evidence; key message in the first linesDisconnected from the analysis
