Why this course?

Numbers are weapons in geopolitics. Governments publish them, hide them and bend them; think tanks, journalists and international organisations fight over them. This course teaches you to use numbers yourself, and to see when they are being used against you.

Why it matters for your career

Analysts in think tanks, international organisations, ministries, consultancies and the economic-security teams of large firms spend much of their time doing three things: finding and checking data, turning it into an argument, and explaining it to people who have five minutes. Most of them learned the methods on the job. This course gives you the core of that toolkit in about 18 hours, with no statistics background required.

You will not learn every technique. You will learn the handful that does most of the work in policy analysis, and the habit of asking three questions of every number: compared with what, measured how, and what could make it misleading?

Who it is for

Students and young professionals in international affairs, management, political science or geopolitics who want to work with evidence, not just read it. You need a laptop, a web browser and curiosity. Everything is free.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the course you will be able to:

  1. Judge whether a number can be trusted: who produced it, how it was measured, and who had an interest in it. (session 1)
  2. Build indicators and draw honest charts: shares of GDP, real terms, growth rates, and charts that do not mislead. (sessions 1, 3)
  3. Read uncertainty in survey data: compute a margin of error and decide whether a difference between groups or years is real. (session 2)
  4. Measure economic dependence and build, map and stress-test a composite index. (session 3)
  5. Estimate and interpret a regression with controls, explain outliers, and say why it shows association rather than cause. (session 4)
  6. Evaluate a policy with difference-in-differences and an event study, and state the assumptions behind the estimate. (session 5)
  7. Cost a geopolitical shock with a range rather than a single number, and audit an AI assistant's analysis. (session 6)
  8. Communicate findings in a two-page policy brief and a three-minute presentation. (sessions 2–6)

How the course works

  • Six sessions of about three hours, at your own pace. Do them in order: each builds on the previous one.
  • Every session has the same rhythm: a puzzle on which you record a guess; two blocks of lesson, activity and feedback; and a final task.
  • Final tasks: a practice exercise (session 1), the choice of your brief question (session 2), the first chart of your brief (session 3), a claim audit (session 4), an evaluation note (session 5), and the finished brief and presentation (session 6).
  • Before each session, read the short reading listed at the bottom of its page (about 30 minutes).
  • Your answers and your brief are saved in this browser only: nothing is sent anywhere. Use the same browser throughout.
